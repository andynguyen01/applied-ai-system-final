PET CARE KNOWLEDGE BASE

1. Feeding consistency matters. Dogs and cats benefit from regular meal times because predictable routines support digestion and reduce stress.
2. Medication is always high priority. If a veterinarian prescribed a dose, it should be given on time unless a vet says otherwise.
3. Water should always be available. Hydration is especially important before and after exercise or in warm environments.
4. Exercise should match the species, age, and energy level of the pet. Dogs usually need structured daily movement, while cats often benefit from short interactive play sessions.
5. Avoid intense activity immediately after a large meal for some dogs, because that can be uncomfortable.
6. Smaller, more frequent meals may work better for some cats than one large meal.
7. Short essential tasks can be scheduled earlier in the day when time is limited.
8. When two tasks overlap, prioritize medication, feeding, and hydration before flexible enrichment.
9. Multi-pet schedules should avoid unnecessary overlap so each pet gets focused attention.
10. Older pets may need shorter and more frequent activity breaks than younger pets.
11. Behavioral changes such as low appetite, low energy, or agitation can signal that the care plan needs adjustment.
12. Routine and predictability reduce stress for many pets.
13. Optional enrichment such as training or extra play should come after core health tasks are complete.
14. Schedule conflicts should be explained in plain language so the owner understands why one task was prioritized.
15. Best-practice scheduling keeps the highest-risk task first, then the shortest essential task, then flexible activities.
