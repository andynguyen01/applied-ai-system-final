PET SCIENCE SUPPLEMENT

Dog guidance:
- Dogs generally benefit from consistent meal times because routine supports digestion and reduces stress.
- Many dogs do best with exercise before long idle periods, but intense activity immediately after a large meal can be uncomfortable.
- Water should be available before and after exercise.
- High-energy dogs often need more structured walks or play than low-energy dogs.
- Medication schedules should be followed exactly when a vet has prescribed them.
- If a dog is older, shorter and more frequent movement breaks can be easier than one long session.

Cat guidance:
- Cats often do well with smaller, more frequent feeding patterns.
- Interactive play is useful for indoor cats and helps support physical and mental stimulation.
- Cats may prefer predictable routines and quiet recovery periods after active play.
- Fresh water should be easy to reach at all times.
- Medication tasks should be treated as time-sensitive when prescribed.
- Overly long or forced activity sessions can stress some cats.

Multi-pet scheduling guidance:
- When multiple pets share a schedule, prioritize health-critical tasks first, then flexible enrichment.
- If two tasks overlap, choose the one with medication, feeding, or hydration importance before optional play.
- Separate high-attention tasks for different pets when possible so each animal gets focused care.
- Shorter essential tasks can be placed earlier in the day to reduce backlog risk.
- Conflict explanations should name the pets and the type of care involved so the owner understands why the overlap matters.
